Fragrance of Soil and Village Fair

A Heart-Touching and Entertaining Village Tale

'Shivapur' was a small village nestled in a tranquil and picturesque corner of Maharashtra. Far away from the dust and hustle of the city, hidden in the lap of hills, this village looked like a beautiful piece of art crafted by nature. The refreshing fragrance of wet earth early in the morning, the melodious clanging of bells around cattle's necks, and the elders sipping tea while sitting on village platforms and chatting away—this was the everyday routine of the village.

A young man named Malhari lived in this very village. Malhari worked in an IT company in the city, but his heart always remained rooted in his village. Whenever the stress of city life piled up, he would rush straight back home. For him, the soil of the village, that ancient banyan tree, and his childhood friends were the true essence of happiness. This summer vacation, he had come back home because the annual village deity 'Bhairavnath Fair' was just a few days away.

The Festive Atmosphere in the Village

A week before the fair, a unique enthusiasm takes over the village. Cleaning of houses, pristine white rangoli patterns drawn right at the thresholds, and the hustle and bustle of buying new clothes can be seen everywhere. Malhari's childhood friend 'Budha' farmed right in the village. Budha was like a walking encyclopedia of village news; from what was growing in whose field, to the market rate of donkeys, right down to which new potter family had recently arrived—Budha had all the gossip. When Malhari arrived, Budha hugged him and said, "So you've come from the city after a long time? Did you forget us?" Malhari smiled and replied, "Oh Budha, no matter how big the city is, the peace I find here cannot be found anywhere else!"

That evening, a large village meeting was held at the Chavadi (village community hall). Discussions were underway about what special highlights to feature in this year's fair. Patil Tataysaheb, an 80-year-old elder with the fiery energy of a youth and strict discipline, quieted everyone and said, "Lads, the fair is our culture. This year's fair must feature traditional games like wrestling arenas, bullock cart races, and a grand late-night Gondhal program of Malhari Maharaj. But alongside this, the youth of our village must also work together to manage village cleanliness and water supplies."

Tamasha: Old Memories and a New Twist

The traditional Tamasha performance was scheduled to be set up just like every year. City kids might not appreciate Tamasha, but for village folks, it's the biggest celebration of the year. People from neighboring hamlets used to flock just to hear the songs of the famous Lavani dancer 'Chandrabhaga' and witness the poetic duels of traditional Shahirs. Malhari and Budha took full responsibility for the fair's planning. The age of social media had reached the village too. Malhari created a digital portal named 'wintask.in' for the youth of the village, which provided live updates of the fair, digital scoreboards, and schedules of sports events. This successfully blended the village's old cultural heritage with modern technology.

The main day of the fair dawned. Since early morning, streams of devotees flooded the temple. The entire village of Shivapur echoed with the thunderous beats of Dhol-Tasha. The marketplace was packed with toy shops, hot pans of jalebis, balloons, and giant wheels. Children were bursting with boundless joy. While walking around, Malhari reached the center of the village fair, where despite the scorching sun, people were joyfully greeting one another. There he met his grandmother, who lovingly stroked his head and said, "Son, no matter how educated or successful you become, never leave the company of your soil."

A Mysterious Turn and True Happiness

Around afternoon, a small incident occurred. The village sarpanch's lost antique silver Samai (traditional oil lamp), which was traditionally used inside the temple sanctum, suddenly went missing. This caused a wave of panic throughout the village. Since the theft happened on the day of the fair, everyone grew anxious. Some claimed it was the work of outside thieves, while others started whispering amongst themselves. Malhari and Budha took the matter into their own hands. Using their modern tech-savvy approach, they checked the village CCTV footage (which had recently been installed by the Gram Panchayat).

Upon reviewing the footage, it turned out that the lamp wasn't stolen at all. A shy, poor village boy named 'Gopal', who while cleaning the temple feared he might drop and break it, wanted to safely dispose of or hide it, but out of panic ended up stashing it away. When Malhari kindly called Gopal over and asked him gently, the boy burst into tears. Malhari brought the truth out in front of everyone and announced that the sarpanch's lamp was completely safe. This averted a major crisis, and everyone praised Malhari's cleverness.

Evening and Conclusion of the Fair

As evening fell, the entire village lit up with brilliant illumination and the warm glow of earthen lamps. The Tamasha tent came alive, and the entire village danced to the rhythmic beats of the Shahir's dholki. Standing in the middle of that vibrant crowd, Malhari soaked in the beauty of his village. He realized that true happiness doesn't lie on velvet sofas, but rather in these smiling faces and affectionate relationships.

This fair wasn't just a festival; it was a thread that bound people together. Late at night when everyone returned to their homes, Malhari made up his mind that starting next year, he would stay right in the village and work towards its development. The timeless, authentic fragrance of the soil remained permanently etched in his heart.
